TASK 2 - Plan the requirement collection
================================================================================

2.1 The five techniques - source and expected learning

Interview         | Department head, operator and accountant | Approval rules, payment handling, roles, exceptions and required reports.
Questionnaire     | At least 100 students                    | File types, colour demand, expected volume and preferred notifications.
Observation       | Existing print room during busy periods  | Actual service steps, bottlenecks, reprints, queue size.
Document analysis | Student lists, price rules, receipts     | Identity fields, accounting evidence, semester dates, retention rules.
Prototyping       | Operator and 5 students                  | Whether submission, queue and cancellation screens are understandable.

2.2 The technique I would use first

Interview the operator. The operator performs the daily process and can quickly expose
exceptions and practical constraints. Observation should follow immediately, to verify that
the described process matches reality.

2.3 Eight interview questions (grouped under three headings)

Money and prices:
1. How and when does a student pay for pages beyond the free allowance?
2. Are unused free pages carried to the next semester?
3. When is a refund or a free reprint allowed?

Jobs and documents:
4. Which file formats and maximum file size must be accepted?
5. May a job mix colour and black-and-white pages?
6. What should happen to a paid job that is never collected?

Users and operation:
7. How is a student identified and who may correct a page balance?
8. How should the queue work when the printer or the network is unavailable?

4.3 The most dangerous omission

Charging a student for a job that failed during printing. If a defect for this is found
only after deployment, students pay for pages they never receive, disputes and refund
requests flood the operator on the first week, and the department loses trust in a service
that was supposed to be simple. Per the lecture cost chart, this defect costs about 1 unit
to close in the requirements phase, roughly 5-10 in design or coding, and 50-100 units
after release, because payment flow, database records, operator procedures and printed
receipts all have to be reworked at once.

5.2 Feasibility answer

Yes, but only for a small version 1 with the core jobs above and existing university
infrastructure. The answer depends mainly on SCHEDULE feasibility - one programmer has
6 weeks, so the scope must be frozen - and on TECHNICAL feasibility of document page
counting and printer integration; economic and operational feasibility are supported by
using the existing accounts and one small room.
